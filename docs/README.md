# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README provides a concise summary of OctoAcme's project management processes and direct links to the full documents in the `docs/` folder. It is intended to help new contributors, stakeholders, and teammates quickly understand the project lifecycle, roles, communication rhythm, and quality expectations across OctoAcme work.

OctoAcme follows a customer-first, iterative delivery model. Projects begin with initiation, where the team validates the business problem, aligns stakeholders, and defines the project charter or one-pager with high-level scope, timeline, and success metrics. Once the initiative is approved, planning turns the idea into a concrete backlog with prioritized work, estimates, acceptance criteria, and a Definition of Done. Execution then moves work through a repeatable delivery cadence using sprint or iteration planning, daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, and issue-based tracking in the project board. After the work is complete, the team moves to release and verification to confirm quality, deployment readiness, and stakeholder communication before closing the effort with a retrospective focused on learning and continuous improvement.

The project relies on clear ownership and shared working agreements across key personas. Product managers define the customer problem and success criteria, project managers coordinate schedules, dependencies, communication, and risks, developers build and test the functionality, QA validates acceptance and quality expectations, and stakeholders provide inputs, approvals, and business context. Communication is intentionally regular and structured: daily standups for delivery progress, weekly PM/PdM alignment, stakeholder updates on a fixed cadence, and escalation paths when risks or blockers need visibility. This creates transparency without overloading the team with unneeded meetings while ensuring decisions and status remain visible to the right people.

Quality is embedded into delivery rather than treated as a final step. Teams plan for technical and operational quality by defining acceptance criteria, using the Definition of Done, tracking dependencies in the risk register, and running the appropriate set of tests and checks before release. OctoAcme expects unit, integration, and smoke testing where relevant; security and linting checks in CI; manual QA for acceptance verification when needed; and release readiness reviews to confirm the value being shipped is safe, measurable, and aligned with customer needs. The process also encourages reflection and learning through retrospectives so the team captures lessons, improvement actions, and better ways to work in future cycles.
